The business question

What time-phased production plan can be generated from the given demand, capacity and material conditions? After changing load- and waiting-related objectives, did demand coverage, load or shortages change? Planners need to inspect both physical quantities and how the plan is evaluated.

Evidence from an integrated sample

These figures describe a particular dataset and configuration. They are not a promise of customer outcomes. Quantity coverage, delivery performance and equipment utilization require separate checks.

The aggregate business quantities stayed the same, while weighted job_bias changed with its weight. That score change alone does not demonstrate a production improvement. Matching aggregates also do not prove that every task assignment stayed identical; detailed results are needed for that assessment.

Keep three distinctions in view

100% quantity coverage does not establish on-time delivery. Two weights changed together, so this comparison cannot isolate either one's effect. Time and load fields must be interpreted in the dataset's units, not relabeled as minutes without conversion.

Apply the approach to your own planning

  • Check that order, work-center, route, material and time identifiers connect correctly.
  • Separate mandatory conditions from soft constraints that can be expressed through a penalty.
  • Keep an approved baseline and, where possible, change one factor per comparison.
  • Review detailed plans and business measures with the planner responsible for accepting changes.
