The business question

How can changeover preferences be expressed when different products run consecutively? This example compares sequences after adjusting color- and container-capacity-related weights. It shows how to inspect trade-offs, rather than assuming that one adjustment will improve every measure.

In this recorded comparison, changeover counts increased after the weights were raised. A feasible plan found within the available resources is not necessarily a proven global optimum. Scores calculated under different weights are also not an improvement percentage. Review sequences, business measures and constraint satisfaction together.

Use consistent evaluation definitions

Weighted cost includes model-specific evaluation terms and cannot be reconstructed by simply multiplying the displayed counts by the weights. Total API request time and the solver time limit measure different things. Because two attribute weights changed together, this comparison does not isolate either attribute's causal effect.

What should the next comparison change?

  • Identify the changeover type you want to reduce and confirm attribute meanings and units.
  • Hold data and runtime resources constant; compare one preference change at a time.
  • Inspect adjacent jobs and cycle transitions to locate the changes.
  • Consider delivery, output and shop-floor conditions before keeping the result, adjusting again or returning to the baseline.
